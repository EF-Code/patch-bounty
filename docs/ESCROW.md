# Testnet escrow design

This is a prototype design for a trusted verifier, not decentralized adjudication. All balances are in yoctoNEAR, and deadlines are NEAR block timestamps in nanoseconds. A creator selects a verifier, a SHA-256 challenge specification digest, and a future deadline. The attached deposit consists of the reward plus a fixed non-refundable storage allowance of 0.02 NEAR. The allowance stays in the contract; it is not part of the reward. The contract keeps one bounded claim per challenge and replaces rejected claims, but never deletes challenges. Deployment must provision enough balance for the contract's own storage and gas. This is not an audited production storage-pricing model.

The states are `open`, `claimed`, `paying`, `approved`, `paid`, `refunding`, and `refunded`. Before the deadline, a claimant submits a SHA-256 candidate digest and the contract records the caller as recipient. Only the named verifier may reject that claim or approve its exact monotonically increasing claim ID, candidate digest, and a previously unused evidence digest, before the deadline. Evidence digests cannot be reused across challenges in the same contract. Approval schedules a transfer to the recorded claimant, never to a verifier-supplied address. A rejection reopens the challenge; an approved claim can never be rejected or refunded. No model output or digest is independently verified on-chain: the named verifier is trusted to run the stated rules and bind the evidence correctly.

While a transfer is pending, duplicate approvals, retries, and refunds fail. Its private callback marks the challenge `paid` on success or `approved` on failure. Anyone may retry a failed approved payout, always to the same claimant, including after expiry. A creator may request a refund at or after the deadline only if the state is `open` or `claimed`; the pending claim expires. Its private callback marks the challenge `refunded` on success or returns it to `open` on failure, allowing another refund attempt. Callback calls are restricted to the contract itself. If a callback itself fails to execute, the contract remains in its pending state; it cannot safely retry without independently resolving whether the transfer occurred. Approval is prohibited at the exact deadline, while refund is permitted at that boundary. Claim IDs are scoped to their challenge; the verifier must check the contract account, network, challenge specification and actual candidate before approving. Verifier calls are normal signed NEAR transactions, not reusable off-chain signatures.

SHA-256 digests and metadata are public. Use only synthetic, non-sensitive candidates. A digest is not privacy protection, a proof of execution, or an attestation. A wrong or malicious authorized verifier can direct the reward to an ineligible claimant by approving their claim. A malicious claimant can occupy the single claim slot until the verifier rejects it or the challenge expires. Claim submission does not prove authorship: another party can copy or front-run a public candidate digest and take the claim slot. A production design needs a claimant-bound commitment and a secure reveal protocol. The contract does not collect or verify an AI attestation.
